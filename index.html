5 · Drug reference

Osteoporosis medicines: rapid prescribing checks

Generic-drug summaries for doctors and nurses. Expand a medicine, then choose a subsection. Always confirm the exact brand, indication, jurisdiction, current SmPC, local formulary and prescriber authority.

Safety-critical: This reference does not replace the current product information. Doses and renal restrictions can differ by formulation, indication and country. Do not use a class summary to prescribe a specific brand.
Administration and prescribing responsibility

Parenteral and anabolic therapies commonly require specialist initiation or shared-care arrangements. Verify resuscitation arrangements, cold-chain/storage, administration competence, pregnancy precautions and local monitoring pathways where applicable.

6 · Evidence & governance

Source hierarchy and safe use

Recommendations are led by primary society guidelines and current regional guidance. Point-of-care summaries and textbooks provide clinical context but do not silently override a current primary source.

UK and Ireland baseline: SIGN 142 remains useful historically, but is archived/withdrawn. The active ruleset therefore uses NOGG 2024 as the principal UK clinical algorithm, with local Irish/Scottish formularies and licensing checked at implementation.

Material reviewed for this application update
  • BMJ Best Practice: Osteoporosis — point-of-care topic PDF, last updated 21 October 2025; used to cross-check diagnosis, investigations, treatment sequencing and monitoring.
  • NOGG Guidelines + EULAR presentation — used for NOGG risk assessment, VFA triggers, FRAX interpretation, treatment duration, safety monitoring, glucocorticoid and androgen-deprivation pathways. Presentation material was treated as supporting education rather than a replacement for the live guideline.
  • Managing osteoporosis in other groups — historical SIGN-derived evidence on men, premenopausal women, glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis and painful vertebral fractures; retained as contextual evidence, not as the active UK algorithm.
  • Rheumatology Secrets: Metabolic Bone Disease — used for secondary-cause patterns, GIOP context and metabolic-bone mimics including osteomalacia, hypophosphatasia and phosphate-wasting disorders.

Where supplied material conflicts with current primary guidance, the current guideline, product information and local formulary take precedence.

Clinical governance

This prototype requires independent clinical validation, local formulary review, medical-device assessment, accessibility testing and controlled versioning before patient-care use. If there are any issues developing or validating the application, please don’t hesitate to contact us.
